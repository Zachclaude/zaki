How to avoid study-in-China scams

The most common scams targeting students who want to study in China, how to check that an offer is real, and what to do if something feels wrong.

Updated 2026-10-13

Every year, students lose money to people who promise them a place at a Chinese university. Most of these scams follow the same few patterns. If you know them, they're easy to spot.

The short version: universities decide admissions, the China Scholarship Council decides Chinese Government Scholarships, and embassies decide visas. Nobody else can promise you any of them.

The scams I see most often

1. "Guaranteed admission" for a fee

Someone offers a guaranteed place at a well-known university if you pay them first, often with a deadline that's "closing today". No agent can guarantee admission. Only the university decides, after it reviews your application.

2. A "deposit" or "processing fee" for a scholarship

You're told you've been selected for a Chinese Government Scholarship, but you must pay a deposit, a "security fee" or a "registration fee" to keep it. Applying for the Chinese Government Scholarship on campuschina.org is free, and real scholarship offers never ask you to pay to receive them.

3. Fake admission letters and fake visa forms

Some scammers send a convincing PDF admission letter, or even a JW201 or JW202 visa form, after you pay. Embassies check these documents with the university. A fake one gets your visa refused and can get you banned from applying again.

4. Paying into a personal account

You're asked to pay tuition or fees to a personal bank account, a personal WeChat or Alipay account, a money-transfer agent or in cryptocurrency. Universities collect fees in the university's own name, and most international students pay tuition after they arrive and register.

5. Look-alike websites and email addresses

A website copies a university's design, or an "admissions officer" writes from a free email address like Gmail or Outlook. Chinese universities use their own domains, almost always ending in .edu.cn.

6. Offers to fix your documents

Someone offers to "improve" your transcripts, write a bank statement, or sell you an HSK or IELTS certificate. Using fake or bought documents is fraud. It leads to cancelled admission, visa refusal or deportation, even years later.

How to check that an offer is real

  1. Find the university's website yourself. Search for it, or use the link on our university pages, then compare it with the site you were sent. Don't trust a link from the person making the offer.
  2. Email the international admissions office directly at the address on the official website, and ask them to confirm your application or admission.
  3. Only apply on official portals: the university's own admission system, and campuschina.org for the Chinese Government Scholarship.
  4. Check who you're paying. The account should be in the university's name, and the details should match what's on the university's official website or official email.
  5. Remember where documents come from. Admission letters and JW201 / JW202 forms come from the university, by post or through its official system.
  6. Keep your originals. Never post your original passport to an individual.
  7. When in doubt, ask the Chinese embassy or consulate in your country.

What we will never do

  • Promise admission, a scholarship or a visa.
  • Ask you to pay into a personal account.
  • Write your application documents for you, or change your documents.
  • Say we represent a university or the Chinese government.
  • Ask you to upload your passport, transcripts or bank statements on this website.

If you think you've been scammed

  1. Stop paying. Scammers often ask for "one last fee".
  2. Keep everything: messages, emails, receipts and the person's account details.
  3. Tell the university whose name was used, through the contact details on its official website.
  4. Report it to the police in your country, and tell the Chinese embassy or consulate.
  5. Warn others. Tell me too, and I'll add the pattern to this page so fewer people get caught.
